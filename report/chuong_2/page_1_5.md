## 2.1. Field overview and related market

### 2.1.1. Conversational AI development trends
Conversational AI has gone through several important development stages, from simple rule-based systems to virtual assistants using machine learning models, and most recently the era of Large Language Models (LLMs).

In the early stage, assistants such as Siri (Apple), Google Assistant, and Alexa (Amazon) introduced voice-based communication with computers. However, these systems usually work through rigid scripts or single commands ("set a timer", "play music", "ask the weather"). They struggle to maintain long conversation context and have no real ability to automatically learn or recall personal details intelligently unless they are explicitly programmed into the database.

The emergence of Transformer architectures and LLMs such as GPT-4, Gemini, and Claude has completely changed the landscape. Computers' ability for Natural Language Understanding (NLU) and Natural Language Generation (NLG) is now close to human-level performance. Modern AI assistants not only understand commands but also emotions, implications, and complex logical reasoning. The 2026 technology trend points toward conversational AI systems with extremely low latency, expressive natural speech, and especially personalized dynamic memory that helps the assistant deeply understand each user's habits, preferences, and information.

### 2.1.2. Personal Knowledge Management (PKM) market
Personal Knowledge Management (PKM) is the process of collecting, classifying, storing, searching, and sharing an individual's knowledge for work and life. The PKM application market is growing strongly due to remote work, lifelong learning, and increasing information-processing pressure.

Modern PKM applications can be divided into three main generations:
1.  **First generation (Folder-based):** Examples include Evernote and OneNote. They store information as files and linear folder hierarchies. They are easy to start with but quickly become messy and hard to search as notes accumulate.
2.  **Second generation (Graph-based & bi-directional linking):** Examples include Obsidian and Roam Research. They use bidirectional links to connect notes into a knowledge graph similar to how the human brain works. However, this generation requires high organizational skill and a lot of manual linking effort.
3.  **Third generation (AI-powered):** Examples include Notion AI and Mem.ai. They integrate AI to help with text summarization, writing, and intelligent search.

Although third-generation PKM apps are already intelligent, they are still **"Text-First"** systems, meaning keyboard-entered text remains the center. Very few apps address **"Voice-First"** thoroughly, meaning they let users build and retrieve their "Second Brain" entirely through natural and seamless speech.
